Why I did this

I wanted to understand your work properly
before asking to be part of it

Reading a paper and saying you found it interesting is easy. I wanted to know whether I actually understood OPERA, and the only honest test I could think of was to rebuild the encoder from the released weights and see whether it worked.

The whole thing in one slide

The paper, and what I did with it

OPERA, in three sentences

  • Respiratory audio has almost no labelled data, so every group trains a bespoke model on a tiny dataset and nothing transfers.
  • OPERA pretrains encoders on 404 hours of unlabelled coughs, breaths and lung sounds, then evaluates them by freezing them and training a single linear layer on 19 downstream health tasks.
  • It releases three encoders and shows domain-specific pretraining beats general-audio models on 16 of those 19 tasks.

What I did, in three sentences

  • Rebuilt one of the three encoders — OPERA-GT — from scratch in PyTorch, recovering its architecture from the released checkpoint's tensor shapes.
  • Evaluated it on one task, T7, COPD detection from ICBHI lung sounds, with subject-wise splits and controls.
  • Then asked three things the paper does not: what else the embeddings encode, what the model costs to run, and whether a small supervised CNN does better.

Scope, stated up front: 1 of 3 encoders · 1 of 19 tasks · no pretraining — I used their released weights · not benchmarked against their published numbers

1,859 lines of source · 560 lines of tests · 76 tests passing

Choosing the paper5
The theory · 1 of 4

The problem OPERA sets out to solve

Labels are the bottleneck

Training a COPD detector traditionally needs lung recordings that a clinician has already diagnosed. That means recruitment, ethics, spirometry. It is slow and expensive, so respiratory datasets stay tiny — ICBHI has 920 recordings.

Every group then trains a bespoke model on its own small set, reports a number, and nothing transfers.

The move

Separate learning what breathing sounds like from learning what disease sounds like.

The first needs no labels at all, and there are hundreds of thousands of unlabelled coughs and breaths in the world. Do it once, expensively. Everyone else reuses it.

That reusable encoder is the foundation model.

Theory6
The theory · 2 of 4

Two ways to learn without labels

Contrastive — OPERA-CT, CE

Take two crops from the same spectrogram and call them a positive pair; crops from different recordings are negatives. Train the model to pick out the partner from a lineup.

Learns: what makes recordings different from each other — discriminative features.

Generative — OPERA-GT

Chop the spectrogram into patches, hide 70% of them, and reconstruct what was behind the mask.

Learns: what respiratory sound looks like in general — descriptive features, because you cannot redraw what you only vaguely understand.

The paper's results show a near-perfect double dissociation: contrastive wins classification (MRR 0.694), generative wins regression (0.655), and the two almost invert between task groups. I found that the most satisfying thing in the paper — it has a clean theoretical explanation and it generalises well beyond audio.

A single fully-connected layer can barely do anything by itself. That is the point: if it performs well, the credit belongs to the representation, not the classifier. It is a clean measurement of embedding quality.

It is also the honest test of the foundation-model promise — can someone with two hundred labelled examples and no GPU get a good result by reusing your encoder?

19 tasks across 10 datasets. 12 classification (AUROC), 7 lung-function regression (MAE). I worked on exactly one of them — T7, COPD detection from ICBHI lung sounds — which is also, notably, one of the three where the paper reports OPERA losing to a general-audio baseline.

Implementation11
Implementation · 3 of 5

The input shape is derived, not chosen

pos_embed = 1025 = 1024 patches + 1 CLS
patch size = 4 × 4
paper states 64 mel bins
→ (64 / 4) × (n_frames / 4) = 1024
→ n_frames = 256 → 8.192 s clips

An honest gap. A 128 × 128 input also yields 1024 patches. Both load, both run, neither raises an error. I chose 64 × 256 because the paper states 64 mel bins — which makes it much the likelier of the two — but I have not verified it, and if I am wrong the embeddings would be quietly degraded rather than obviously broken. It is the first thing I would check with you.

Why no torchaudio

Its defaults differ subtly from the paper's stated parameters — mel scale variant, normalisation, padding. Depending on them silently would risk a preprocessing mismatch that produces plausible-looking, meaningless embeddings. Writing the filterbank is five lines.

The line that matters most

Per-example standardisation. Log compression turns gain into an additive offset, so subtracting the mean removes recording level entirely. ICBHI's four devices have very different sensitivities — without this, the model would see equipment rather than pathology.

Experiment A16
Experiment C1 · the question

What else, besides disease, do the embeddings carry?

In a contrastive objective built from two crops of the same recording, the device, the subject and the session are all constant within that recording — so they are perfectly predictive of a positive pair.

That observation is what prompted the experiment. A representation encoding nothing but "which recording is this" would achieve zero contrastive loss. So I wanted to see what a linear probe could recover from the frozen features when pointed at variables that should not matter clinically.

Because ICBHI was recorded with four different stethoscopes, it gives a clean device variable — which is the only reason this experiment was possible on public data at all. And because sample rate is confounded with device, I had to run it twice: once naively, and once on a bandwidth-matched subset. The gap between those two is the actual measurement.

Experiment C118
Experiment C1 · the caveats

What this does not show

It is not evidence of shortcut learning

Linear decodability is a necessary condition, not a sufficient one. Showing that device information is present is not the same as showing the COPD classifier uses it. Establishing that would need a cross-device generalisation test — train on some devices, hold others out — which I have not run.

Device is confounded with site

In ICBHI, different clinical sites used different stethoscopes. So some of what my probe is reading may be site or patient population rather than microphone. I cannot separate those with this dataset, and that limits how far the observation generalises.

I am raising this as a question rather than a finding. It seemed relevant given that the product has to work across many different earbuds — but you will know far better than I do whether it is a real concern or something you have already accounted for.

I do not think this should be read as "the foundation model doesn't work"

  • It is not a like-for-like comparison — the probe is linear on frozen features by protocol; the CNN trains end to end. Fine-tuning the encoder would be the fair fight, and I have not run it.
  • The CNN figure is a single split with best-epoch selection, which is optimistic. The probe figure is a mean over eight resampled splits.
  • The CNN may well be exploiting the same device structure my earlier probe found — possibly more effectively than the frozen features do.

The most I would claim is: on this task, at this data scale, under the linear-probe protocol, the pretraining did not pay for itself. Which is consistent with T7 being one of the tasks the paper itself reports OPERA losing.

Experiment C321
A mistake I made

My first run reported zero variance on every single probe

It looked like remarkable stability. It was nothing of the sort — logistic regression fitted on fixed data is deterministic, so varying the classifier's random seed varies nothing at all. My error bars were describing solver noise, which is zero.

The uncomfortable part is that I had written a note to myself, a few days earlier, observing that reporting variance across probe seeds rather than across data splits would understate uncertainty — and then implemented exactly that. I only caught it because the zeros were conspicuous.

Patient-leakage inflationOne splitEight resampled splits
AUROC difference+0.004+0.066

A sixteen-fold difference. The single split had landed somewhere the leakage effect was invisible. I am including this because it changed my conclusions more than any of my results did.

What went wrong22
Honest limitations

What this work does not support

  • One encoder of three. GT only — and, as noted, the one my own reasoning said was least likely to show the device effect.
  • One task of nineteen.
  • Not benchmarked against your numbers. The task definition is unmatched.
  • Input orientation unverified. 128 × 128 is also consistent with the checkpoint.
  • Head count inferred from ViT-Small convention, not read from anywhere.
  • One 8.2 s clip per recording, centred. Recordings run up to 86 seconds, so most of the audio is discarded.
  • No fine-tuning comparison.
  • Latency measured on a laptop, not on anything resembling a wearable.

The honest label for all of this is: a verified reimplementation of the OPERA-GT encoder, and an independent evaluation of it on one dataset — not a replication of your benchmark. I have tried to be careful to say that everywhere it appears.

Limitations23
Where I would go next

If I were carrying this further

  • 01Match your T7 definition and re-run. A few hours, since the features are cached — and it is the prerequisite for any comparison at all.
  • 02Cross-device generalisation. Train on some stethoscopes, test on held-out ones. This turns "device is decodable" into "device causes failure", which is the claim that would actually matter.
  • 03Probe OPERA-CT. My reasoning predicts the contrastive model should encode device more strongly than the generative one. GT already scores high, so testing CT would say whether this is objective-driven or inherent to spectrogram encoders.
  • 04Profile OPERA-CE. At around 4M parameters, if it is much faster at comparable accuracy, the practical recommendation for a wearable might invert the headline.
  • 05A fair fight for the baseline — same eight splits, validation-based stopping.
